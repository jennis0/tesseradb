## What the migration was

The directory's declarations predated the configuration surface — `listing`, `values_key`,
`ungated`, `visible_when`, `artifacts_carry_own`, `content.derived`, a separate `layers.toml`, a
`kind = "label_text"` supplied content — and its files were the earlier grain: one
`artifacts.parquet` and one `members.parquet` each carrying a `layer` column, one row per
`(artifact, rank)`, and the access relation as an exploded `pairs.parquet` beside a `terms.parquet`
dictionary.

The notebook's writer had already moved to the current form (one file per layer, one row per
artifact with `contents` as the ranking, `categories` as a list column on the points,
`[layer.labels]` for each label set, one `schema.toml`); the files on disk had not. They were
reshaped in place to exactly what the writer produces, without re-running the notebook: the
per-layer files split out of the two shared ones, the `categories` column rejoined from
`corpus.parquet` on the arXiv id and checked equal to the exploded relation (86,025 pairs both
ways), the vocabularies' `label` column renamed `title`, and the declaration generated by
executing the writer's own template. The old files are deleted. `manifest.json` records the
reshaping.

One edit to the writer itself: `content.computed` on the two clusterings gains `hull`, which the
explorer draws. Every computed property is recomputed per viewer from `membership ∩ M_auth`, so
a hull discloses nothing a box did not. The declaration passes `tessera check` clean: 12 sources,
1 view, 2 vocabularies, 6 attributes, 5 layers.

**One thing the reshaping did not change:** the sample's coordinates are the ones the old notebook
wrote, already scaled by hand onto 0…65,536, where the writer now emits raw UMAP coordinates and
lets `extent = "auto"` frame them. `auto` frames the scaled ones just as well — the build reports
the data spanning 50,802 × 64,252 cells of the grid and nothing clamped — so the map is right;
only the numbers on the axes differ from what a fresh run would show.

## What the service publishes

`/v1/meta` names the five layers with their shape:

| layer | hierarchy | `depends_on` | levels | computed | supplied |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clusters/kmeans` | flat | — | — | centroid, box, hull | — |
| `clusters/hdbscan` | nested | — | — | centroid, box, hull | — |
| `taxonomy/arxiv` | tiered | — | 0 *archive*, 1 *subject class* | centroid, box | — |
| `topics/kmeans` | flat | `clusters/kmeans` | — | — | text |
| `topics/hdbscan` | flat | `clusters/hdbscan` | — | — | text |

The artifacts frame carries `layer, tessera_id, key, masked_count, centroid_x/y, box_*, hull_x/y,
content, parent_id`.

**A `k = 0` viewport naming `clusters/hdbscan` with `artifact_budget: 20`** returns a cut of 19
artifacts, 18 of them carrying a `parent_id` that resolves in the same response; at budget 5 it
is 3 artifacts. Without a budget the full principal is served all 263, with `parent_id`s naming
the whole tree.

**The labels carry text and their target's count** (S3's D13 ruling — a count, not an
identifier). Asked for `clusters/hdbscan` and `topics/hdbscan` together, the full principal gets
263 clusters and 263 labels, and **263 of 263 labels carry exactly their cluster's masked count**;
each label's `content` is its topic text (`qcd galaxies field`, or `(no distinctive terms)` where
the TF-IDF ranking found none). No column names the target: the client matches a label to its
cluster by the count and the centroid it is placed at, as the design says.

## Per-layer served counts under five principals

Served artifacts, by principal, whole extent, no budget:

| principal | visible | `clusters/kmeans` (floor 50) | `clusters/hdbscan` (floor 5%) | `taxonomy/arxiv` (floor 1) | `topics/kmeans` | `topics/hdbscan` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| full — 173 terms | 50,000 | 64 | 263 | 194 | 64 | 263 |
| heavy — 15 terms | 25,210 | 64 | 263 | 178 | 0 | 0 |
| medium — 2 terms | 7,492 | 61 | 263 | 131 | 0 | 0 |
| sparse — 2 terms | 981 | 0 | 4 | 64 | 0 | 0 |
| narrow — `patt-sol` | 7 | 0 | 0 | 8 | 0 | 0 |

Two things in that table are worth knowing before demonstrating with it.

- **The two floors behave as declared, and differently.** The absolute floor drops k-means
  clusters one at a time as a principal narrows (64 → 61 → 0); the proportional floor holds the
  whole HDBSCAN tree for any principal seeing a fair share of everything (a uniform 15% sample
  clears 5% of every cluster) and then collapses to the four clusters a two-category principal
  concentrates in. The sparse principal is the one to show: four clusters out of 263, and the cut
  under a budget of 20 is those same four.
- **The labels are served only to the full principal.** Each topic's text is declared
  `require_member_visibility = "all"` on the content — it is a synthesis of 200 sampled titles —
  and a principal holding 50% of the corpus holds all 200 of none of them. That is the
  declaration doing what it says (a viewer reads a synthesis only where it can read every
  document it was drawn from), and it means the label layers show nothing under any preset but
  *full*. A demo that wants labels under narrower principals needs either a smaller generating
  set per label (`LABEL_SAMPLE` in the notebook — drawn within one cluster, so a principal
  concentrated in a cluster could hold it) or `inherited` content, which is the wrong
  declaration for generated text. Recorded, not changed: it is the notebook's choice, and the
  brief's.

## What changed outside the data

- `run_demo.sh` knows a `notebook` scale: its declaration is the notebook's own `schema.toml`
  (every source relative to it), it takes no `--limit` (its files are one corpus), its item count
  is read off the points file's footer rather than tabled, and its presets are measured over the
  corpus's own vocabulary — the arXiv categories, ranked by pair count into the same
  `[{term, pairs}]` shape `scripts/rank_terms.py` produces, so the one measuring script composes
  the same five bands over both dictionaries.
- The dataset document is now written in `--no-viewer` runs too, and **merged** rather than
  overwritten: an entry another invocation wrote is kept while its viewer plane answers `/readyz`
  and dropped when it does not, so `--scale 2m4` in one terminal and `--scale notebook` in
  another leave a picker offering both.
- `TESSERA_BIN` serves a binary built elsewhere, so a second worktree does not rebuild the crate.

## The whole corpus through the same pipeline — `notebook-2m4`

Added 2026-08-26, at the owner's ask that every layer be tested at 2.4M. `data/notebook-2m4-live/`
is the full 2,422,486-paper corpus through the same notebook pipeline (UMAP computed there rather
than reused), with a fourth clustering the sample does not have: `clusters/toponymy`, four tiered
levels of 16 / 46 / 161 / 574 topics named by a language model from exemplar titles, and a label
layer over it. Its declaration was already in the current form (the writer wrote it); the one edit
is `hull` on the three clusterings, as on the sample. `tessera check`: 16 sources, 1 view, 2
vocabularies, 6 attributes, 7 layers. `./run_demo.sh --scale notebook-2m4` serves it on viewer
37590, session 49308, control 45726.

`topics/toponymy` attaches to `clusters/toponymy` — every one of its 797 artifacts names that
layer, and their generating sets are the exemplars per topic (11,955 member rows over 797 labels,
about six each) — so it is declared as that layer's `[layer.labels]`, not the HDBSCAN layer's.

At this scale the proportional floor bites where it did not on the sample: a two-category
principal at 15% is served 146 of 257 HDBSCAN clusters, not all of them. And the toponymy labels
are the ones to demonstrate labels with: a generating set of six exemplars is small enough that a
50% principal holds every document behind 243 of 797 names, where the TF-IDF topics' 200-title
sets are held by nobody but the full principal (one exception under *heavy* — a cluster's second,
smaller-set fallback text).
