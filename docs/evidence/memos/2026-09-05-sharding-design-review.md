# Review: epoch shards in one process (sharding.md with decisions 0126 and 0127)

Reviewed 2026-09-05 against the design as amended today, the four probes of 2026-09-04, the ingest campaign's figures and the documents the brief names. Nothing is built; nothing was run.

## Verdict

**Is it the right design?** For the entity side, yes. Allocation, postings, attributes, the transpose, the overlay, the free pool and the entity passes of compaction partition cleanly by epoch, and nothing else partitions them at all: a key hash cannot serve reuse-before-grow, and a spatial key is per view. Making each layer incarnation its own entity space (decision 0127) is also right; I found no join that ever used the shared space as a key (table below), and the one thing the shared space did, count an artifact's attribute value as an item, was an accident that ruling K removes.

For the row side it is right for tiles and wrong for artifact memberships. A tile fans out N ways but the work is batchable per shard, so the request path closes at N=100 if, and only if, every per-tile operation is batched the way `count_ranges` is. An artifact-major level does not batch: every compact artifact has members in every epoch, so its row form is one container per (artifact, shard) and the fixed cost per container is paid N times over. That is the product the owner asked about, and it does not close at N=100 for a level of 10⁷ artifacts. It closes at tens.

**Is it sufficient for the target?** Not as written. Five things stand between the document and tens-to-a-hundred shards in one process, in order of consequence: the artifact row-form product; a compaction that is per shard but still costs 2.7 hours per 2³⁰-row shard against gauges that fire hourly; a layer publish that resolves every member through N locators and rebuilds N×V structures inline on the executor; a request path that names one batched operation where four are needed; and a session path whose cost at the target the document does not state. All five are fixable inside the epoch design; none requires changing the axis. The two I would change before S1 are the seal size (pick it from the read side, at the entity ceiling, so N is about 25 at 10¹¹) and the shape of compaction (two tiers, so the cheap tier runs at the deletion cadence and the expensive tier at the reuse cadence).

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The artifact row-space product does not close at N=100

**What breaks.** Under epoch shards a compact artifact has members in every shard, so its row form, its tile-index extent and its containment slice exist once per shard. The per-container constant is what the whole artifact-side cost rests on, and it is per (artifact, shard): about 90 B resident per Roaring container (measured, `probes/2026-08-16-membership-residency/`), and the tile-index extent at exactly N × 8 B per ordinal (measured at N=8, 8.83× bytes, `probes/2026-09-04-epoch-shard-tile-index/`). The row form of a 10⁷-artifact level over 10⁹ rows is 3.6 GB resident today at about four runs per artifact (measured, annotation-representation §11.3). At N=100 each artifact holds at least one container per shard, so the same level is at least 10⁷ × 100 × 90 B ≈ 90 GB per (view, level) resident when composed, and about 20 GB serialised against under 1 GB today. My estimate from the measured constants; the tile-index probe measured the extent column and candidacy and did not measure row forms. On the request path the same product appears as one masked probe per (candidate, shard): a wide viewport on a fine level already spends 549 of 553 ms in candidacy at 10⁷ over 10⁸ (measured, artifact-serving-at-scale §7.4), and each candidate now owes N probes. The design's shared `everywhere` set removes the N× repetition of the wide artifacts and leaves the placed ones exactly where the probe found them: 6 to 8× at N=8, hash rule, on both k-means layers.

**At what scale.** Tens of shards and 10⁶ artifacts: tolerable (rung 5's million artifacts at N=8 add under a gigabyte by the same arithmetic; its 16 GB anon floor is the DAG's memberships, which are per member and do not multiply). A hundred shards and 10⁷ artifacts: not.

**Why the axis cannot simply be swapped for row space.** A Morton-range row shard keeps a compact artifact in one shard, which is what scaling-analysis §4 assumed and what would restore today's figures. But an epoch's permutation maps entity to row, and a compaction of one range shard renumbers rows belonging to every epoch, so every epoch's permutation is rewritten at every range-shard compaction: about N × 4 GB of scattered permutation writes per compaction against 4 GB in the epoch design, and 100 compactions per cycle. My estimate. That is the write-path cost the owner asked for and it is why I do not recommend the two-axis layout.

**What I would take instead.** Two things. Seal at the entity ceiling rather than 2³⁰, so N is about 25 at 10¹¹ rows: every fan-out cost falls 4×, and the per-shard costs that rise 4× are the ones finding 2 makes cheap. And measure row-form residency and probe count at N=8 and N=32 on rung 5 before S5, which is the gate the design should carry instead of "the tile-index probe re-run at N=8". If a 10⁷-artifact level over 10¹¹ rows is a real target, say in artifact-serving-at-scale that it is served row-major only under sharding and price the label column per shard (4 GB per (shard, view, level), N-independent per row, so 100 GB at 10¹¹; also not small).

### 2. Compaction is per shard but its cost and its gauges do not meet at the target

**What breaks.** One real compaction measured 330 s at 36M rows (`probes/2026-09-04-epoch-shard-fold-decomposition/`), and the design's own decomposition makes 50.5% of it linear in the shard's rows and 48.1% linear in the shard's memberships. Scaled linearly to a 2³⁰-row shard that is about 2.7 hours per compaction, of which the attribute and transpose passes are 73 minutes and the row space, postings, external ids and digests are 9 minutes. Modelled from one loaded run; class A's per-row figures are the most exposed to that load. Against that, the trigger gauges become per shard and `compaction_after_deletions` (500,000, unwindowed) fires per shard. At 1% daily churn over 10¹¹ rows a shard takes 10⁷ deletions a day and crosses the gauge twenty times a day; at 0.01% churn, once every five days per shard, which is twenty compactions a day across the fleet at 2.7 hours each, serially, one shard at a time by ruling. The dead-rows gauge at 0.2 is better behaved (five compactions a day at 1% churn) and still needs 13.5 hours of the day. Reuse-before-grow guarantees every shard takes deletions, so no shard is exempt.

**What I would take instead.** Two tiers, since 88% of the shard-linear cost is the two passes whose only purpose under reuse is to let the slot return. A row tier (row space, postings, external ids, membership slices, digests: 9 minutes at 2³⁰) that removes dead rows and retires deletions under Rule F, and returns no slot. An entity tier (attributes, transpose) that runs at the dead-bytes cadence and returns the slots the row tier freed, which is decision 0072's reconcile-before-reclaim order made a cadence. The row tier fits a window; the entity tier at 1% churn runs about once a day fleet-wide. Whether an orphaned attribute value between the tiers is reachable I checked: the entity leaves the postings at the row tier, so no fragment names it and every filter meets the mask first. Also raise `compaction_after_deletions` per shard in proportion to the seal size, or the gauge fires continuously at any churn.

### 3. A layer publish at the target is inline, hours long, and pays N locators per member

**What breaks.** Three things the document does not price. (a) Every member of every published or grown artifact resolves its key through the shard locators, and there is no global key-to-shard structure: after a closing compaction that is one binary search per shard per member, about 3 µs each, so 300 µs per member at N=100 (my estimate). The layers stage today runs at about 4 × 10⁶ memberships a second (6:52 for 1.66 × 10⁹ at rung 3, measured); at 300 µs a member a 10⁹-member level takes days. The same per-row cost lands on ingest's duplicate check (write-path §2.1 step 7), which becomes 100 searches per row. (b) A publish rebuilds the level's row forms and tile index for every (view, shard), and today that work runs on the executor thread inside publication (probe row 11: 135 s for 1.66 × 10⁹ memberships, single-threaded, blocking flush). At 10¹¹ memberships that is hours on the executor. (c) The one-shard-at-a-time publication story holds for compaction and not for a layer publish, which writes into every shard directory at once and makes every shard's derived structures for that level stale in one flip.

**What I would take instead.** Publish a layer per shard, as N publications, using decision 0127b's growth semantics: an artifact under publication is an artifact under ingest, so a level whose memberships have landed in 40 of 100 shards discloses nothing. That makes (b) and (c) incremental and puts the projection on the pool. For (a), a global key-to-shard filter: a Bloom filter per shard at 10 bits per entity is 1.3 GB per 2³⁰ shard, page-cache resident, one probe per shard per key at about 100 ns, so 10 µs a row at N=100 rather than 300; or a bundle-wide keyed run written at each flush beside the per-shard ones. Either way the design should state that per-shard locators multiply every key resolution by N, because that multiplication lands on the side that dominates cost today.

### 4. The request path closes at N=100 only if everything is batched per shard, and the design names one operation

**What it costs.** From the treemap probe's per-part constants (measured at N=8, one thread): count 2.2 µs per part on a bitset container, called twice per part; decode about 0.2 µs; select about 0.3 µs. A 3,000-tile request at N=100 is 300,000 parts: about 1.3 s of count and 0.15 s of decode and select single-threaded, about 130 ms on twelve cores. With `count_ranges` the count becomes one container walk per shard plus a rank read per range, about 20 to 50 ms fleet-wide (my estimate; the design says its gain is unmeasured), so the batched request is about 200 ms single-threaded, 20 ms on twelve cores. Interactive, but only with the pool and only if decode, select and artifact candidacy take the same shape. Range resolution already does (`tile_ranges_all` is one gallop sweep per segment), and that is the shape to copy. The artifact probes of finding 1 are not covered by any of this.

**What I would take.** A stated rule in §2: no request-path operation is invoked per (tile, shard); every operation over a request's tiles is one call per shard over the sorted tile ranges. And extend the treemap bench from N=8 to N=32 and N=100 at S2, since every ratio in the probe is already near N× at depth 12 and nothing in the design shows what a hundred parts per tile costs end to end.

### 5. The session path at the target is not stated, and the number is the design's real ceiling

**What it costs.** First touch at zoom 0 needs every shard's leaf and projection. Per 2³⁰-row shard the projection is 1.39 s at 25%, 0.66 s at 10%, 0.24 s at 1% (modelled from the probe's fit, warm permutation). At N=100 that is 139, 66 and 24 s of CPU per session's first whole-map request, plus each shard's fragment union, plus 4 GB of permutation per shard from disc where it is not cached (400 GB per view of permutations at 10¹¹ rows; 1.3 s per shard at 3 GB/s). On twelve cores the 25% case is about 12 s to first paint; on sixty-four, about 2 s. Memory per session per view: above 6.25% coverage every container is a bitset, so a leaf is 128 MB and a session holds 12.8 GB of fragment plus 12.8 GB of projection per view, 25.6 GB; at 1%, 4.3 GB; at 0.1%, 0.43 GB. So a 512 GB box serves about fifteen dense sessions per view, or several hundred sparse ones, before the bundle's own working set. This is the materialised-mask architecture's cost rather than sharding's, and scaling-analysis §4 already states the precondition (sub-linear cardinality); the design lists it as assumed and gives no figure at the target. It should, because it is what decides whether "a few large boxes" is a deployment shape.

**One cheap lever.** `RowProjectionKey` is keyed by `token_id` (cache.rs); fragments are keyed by satisfied terms and so are shared between principals with the same grant, but projections are not. Keying the projection by fragment identity rather than token lets identical principals share the 12.8 GB, which at fifteen sessions a box is worth having.

### 6. The request-aware route in §2.5 fails decision 0124's own admissibility test

§2.5 takes the row-major scan "when the viewport's visible rows are fewer than the level's placed artifacts". The first quantity is the principal's own count, disclosed per tile by §7.1. The second is an unmasked corpus quantity that `/v1/meta` deliberately never serves (artifact-system §6, "never an artifact count"). Timing therefore says which side of an unpublished corpus statistic the principal's count falls on. Decision 0124 admitted a cardinality-driven route on one verb because the constant compared against was "a deployment parameter identical for every principal, not a corpus statistic"; this route compares against a corpus statistic, on the viewport verb, and the design says it adds no register row. Compare against a published constant, or drive the route from the tile count as §2.4 already does for filters.

### 7. Per-shard short-circuits: the design is right that no new row is needed and wrong that nothing needs saying

Places where work varies per shard with the principal, and where each lands:

- A count, decode or projection that skips an empty leaf. Work varies with the principal's own per-shard coverage, which is which epochs hold their visible items. Under reuse-before-grow a replacement's epoch is chosen by where other principals' deletions freed slots, so this is activity on unseen data reaching timing. C4's posture, unquantified residual after the correlation with the viewer's own count; add a sentence there rather than claim silence.
- A `FragmentCache` hit or miss per shard: cross-session, since another session with the same terms may have warmed shard k and not shard j. §8.5 already carries the whole-fragment form; per shard is finer sampling of the same channel. Note it beside §8.5.
- `/v1/items` on an identifier whose shard was dropped, and `/v1/artifacts` on an identifier whose incarnation was dropped. C4's closure on `/v1/items` rests on identical work for "names nothing" and "names an invisible item": three constant-time lookups. A dispatch that finds no shard and returns early breaks that. Both paths must take the same lookups; the design should say so, since it is the one place the register's structural closure depends on an implementation choice.
- The wire stamp. `segments_version` and the overlay version become per shard; decision 0041's stamp is `(prefix, segments_version)`. If the stamp becomes a vector it says which epoch moved, which is per-shard activity on the viewer plane; §5 says nothing per shard reaches that plane. State that the stamp stays the prefix digest.
- Containment "over the shards where G has members", per-shard route choice by tile count, suggest under decision 0124's rule on the sum, the occupancy count and shard field inside the permutation: all sound; none is principal-dependent or reaches the wire.

### 8. An incarnation that is never compacted leaks on exactly the layers whose identity must survive

Decision 0127 says an incarnation has no compaction and a deletion leaves its overlay only at the drop. For a replace-only layer that is right. For a layer whose lifecycle is editing (annotation-representation §5.0.1: selections, corrected labels, redrawn boundaries), edits append record extents that are never rewritten (§2.4's first-matching-layer trap), deleted artifacts leave holes and overlay entries that never retire, their membership slices persist in every shard, and the ordinal cursor is monotone so the level's `u32` space is consumed by creates. Replacement is the only reclaim, and replacement is the operation that kills the bookmarks the edit pass exists to keep. The fix is small: an ordinal-preserving rewrite of an incarnation, which rep §5.0.3 already describes for a level (holes stay holes, so `FPE_k(kind ‖ incarnation ‖ entity)` is untouched), retiring deleted entries under Rule F in the publication that drops their records. "Never compacted" should read "compacted only by a rewrite that preserves every ordinal". On 0081: I found no case for identity surviving a replacement that the incarnation model creates; the case is the edit pass, and the edit pass now needs this rewrite.

One cost the design leaves unnamed: a point-shard compaction that frees slots must reconcile every generating set in every incarnation naming a member in that shard (decision 0072's reconcile-before-reclaim, strict or permissive). That is a walk over every G-bearing artifact per point-shard compaction, N times per cycle rather than once: class C by the probe's classification (1.9 s for 30,473 records' lineage, row 12), so about 10 minutes per compaction at 10⁷ records, my estimate. A shard drop runs the same reconciliation for every G that touches the shard, and under strict that withdraws most corpus-derived content in a deployment whose generating sets span epochs. The drop verb's consequences should say so.

### 9. Reuse-before-grow: the locality cost is bounded, the bound is the scattered posting, and every shard now decays towards it

The design claims the bound is "containers touched". That is the cost model, not a bound on the count. Under scattered deletions the pool is scattered singletons, a window's signature-sorted rows land in whatever slots the pool holds, and a large term's posting in a shard tends towards touching every container of the shard (16,384 at 2³⁰). It saturates there and does not compound past it: a term of |T| entries touches at most min(|T|, 16,384) containers per shard, and the projection's scattered arm is already the figure the projection probe tells the design to use (2× the contiguous arm at 1%). What the design should say is what the saturation costs, because it is measured: signature-sorted postings are 8.9 to 36.7× smaller than scattered and union up to 130× faster at equal coverage (architecture §13.3, r16), and that gap is what a fully churned shard gives back. Sealed-by-default confined the decay to the open shard; reuse everywhere spreads it to every shard, and nothing restores it, since a slot is identity. Close the open question: hand out pool slots in runs of at least a stated length and leave shorter holes to the entity ceiling, which bounds scatter to the run length; and measure the decay on the campaign's f=50% ingest cycle, which already exists.

### 10. What is missing

- **A shard is not independently restorable, and the document implies it is.** Which items belong to shard k is recorded only in shard k's locator, so a lost shard cannot be rebuilt selectively from source; and a shard restored from an older copy carries membership slices keyed by incarnations that may since have been replaced, so its points fall out of every current layer with no error beyond an unknown incarnation number. State the backup unit: a sealed shard with an empty pool and no deletions is byte-stable, and that stability is the backup unit, with the manifest.
- **Open-time verification at the target.** Open digest-checks every listed file (read.rs, `open_bundle`); rung 4 verified 70 GB in 74 s. A bundle of tens of terabytes verifies for hours at every restart. Ruling F needs per-shard verification that runs in parallel, and a recorded verification for stable sealed shards, or a restart is a multi-hour outage.
- **Ruling D contradicts decision 0047 for points.** A point edit is delete plus re-ingest and mints a new identity in whatever shard allocation targets; "edits keep identity, so land in their shard" is true of artifacts only, and §3.2's "any shard for edits that changed geometry or attributes" names a path points do not have.
- **Concurrency of compaction.** "One point shard at a time" is a ruling in §1.1 and finding 2 says it cannot hold at N=100 under any churn. Either concurrent per-shard compactions with a memory budget, or the two tiers, or both.
- **The manifest.** `files` covers every shard and every publication rewrites it; at N=100 with per-shard segments, extents and per-level slices that is tens of thousands of entries rewritten per flush. Not fatal; state its size.
- **The window and the target.** A commit window's signature sort is one shard's; a window that straddles a change of target splits its locality. Minor, but the reuse threshold's default decides how often that happens and §11 leaves it open.
- §3.2 says a build past the seal size writes "shards 0, 2, 3 and so on", which skips 1; a typo or a rule I could not find.

## Tested and found sound

- Priority composes exactly across shards: the identity is a keyed permutation of the whole input, no two items share one (architecture §12.3's argument transfers unchanged).
- θ anchors on the summed unfiltered count, per tile over the union of parts; the three regimes compose as §12.3 states.
- I7: selection runs over the union of parts, never a precomputed structure; the marker stays.
- I3 and I12: containment is an AND of per-shard tests against the authorised leaf; the filter reaches only decision 0104's bit.
- The two removal rules per shard and per incarnation; a drop meets the deletion rule for every entry at once; Rule F's identity-match argument holds per leaf provided composition checks each leaf's identity, which §2.2 implies.
- Shard and incarnation numbers never reused; a dropped shard's identifiers fail whole-identifier equality; decision 0091's equivalence across a seal (fixture f).
- The occupancy count: two occupants of a slot always differ, nothing wraps, retirement at the cap costs one slot per 4,096 reuses; the plaintext prior narrows and decision 0014 already rests no claim on it.
- The kind bit: point and artifact inputs cannot collide; 31 incarnation bits are past any pipeline.
- Ruling K: an artifact's attribute value is no longer an item on point surfaces.
- I13b: every shard is held or the bundle is refused; a generating set cannot name a shard the process lacks.
- WAL replay recovers per-shard allocators, pools and incarnation cursors, since each record carries shard or incarnation and, for a point, generation.
- The histogram walk is 1× under sharding (measured); its cache stays one per token.
- A sealed shard with an empty pool and no deletions is byte-stable; a compaction of shard k hard-links the rest whole.

## Open questions only the owner can answer

- **Seal size.** The design expects 2³⁰ to bound per-shard compaction and projection. Finding 1 says every fan-out cost scales with N and the artifact product with N per artifact; finding 2 makes per-shard compaction cheap by tiering. Is the seal to be chosen from the read side (the entity ceiling, N≈25 at 10¹¹) or the write side?
- **Is 10⁷ artifacts over 10¹¹ rows a target?** If yes, artifact-major serving under epoch shards needs a different construction and the document should say which; if no, say the artifact ceiling the design is sized for.
- **Compaction concurrency.** Two tiers, N in flight, or both.
- **Per-shard layer publication.** Incremental visibility of a level across shards is disclosure-neutral by decision 0127b's argument; is it acceptable operationally?
- **Dense principals at the target.** Fifteen sessions at 25% coverage per 512 GB per view is the number; is that the deployment shape, or does the design owe a per-session budget below the materialised mask (the §7.9 histogram path, or a shared projection per grant) before 10¹¹ is promised?
- **The incarnation rewrite.** Ordinal-preserving compaction of an incarnation, or accept that edit-heavy layers are replaced periodically and lose identity.
