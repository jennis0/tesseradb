# Part 2 facts — entity/row space, Morton ranking, Roaring containers, cost model, on-disk layout and query path

Measurement environment (applies to all probes/results.md and probes/optimisations.md figures below unless a different corpus/config is noted inline): "Environment throughout: WSL2, 12 cores, 39 GB, RTX 3080." "Timings are indicative, not certified — WSL2 was chosen for mmap and page-cache fidelity, not latency." — source: probes/results.md §1 (preamble).

## Entity space vs row space, and the permutation

- Every item has a permanent entity ID, stable across temporal slices and rebuilds, never reused (I9), and never exposed (I10); all permission data is expressed in this space — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.1.
- Entity IDs are allocated in append-only batches; the order within a batch is a free choice, spent deliberately (§11.1) — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.1.
- Each temporal slice separately assigns row IDs by Morton rank (§5.2) — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.1.
- A slice stores a `u32` permutation array mapping entity ID to row ID, sized by maximum live entity ID rather than item count, with a sentinel for absent entities; the inverse direction is not stored separately — it is the entity-ID column of the row-ordered store — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.1.
- With deletions and append-only IDs, the gap between entity-ID count and item count grows, which is the exhaustion concern of §16 — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.1.
- This entity/row factoring is what stops the term index being duplicated per slice: within a partition there is exactly one index, in entity space, shared across all slices; a mask fragment is built once there and permuted into a slice's row space on demand — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.1.
- Masks are built in entity space (I4) but tile ranges are in row space; the naive construction (iterate, look up, insert) is slow because inserts arrive out of order — instead iterate in order, gather `entity_to_row[e]` into a flat buffer, radix sort, and bulk-construct from the sorted array — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Spend the entity-ID ordering on posting compression. Because entity and row space are related only by a permutation, the two orderings can be optimised independently. Row space is fixed by geometry; entity space is free within each append-only batch. Assign entity IDs within a batch sorted by term signature, so term postings form long runs inside each batch's ID range and head terms encode as run containers. It costs nothing, does not weaken I9, and is safe only because of I10." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §11.1 (line 459 region).
- "This is index compactness, not the avoidance of a cliff: a fully scattered mask falls back to fixed-size bitmap containers whose footprint is already the figure in Appendix A." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md (line 463 region, following §11.1).
- Permuting a mask from entity to row space is the expensive step: "8.8 s for a 69M-item mask" — measured at 10⁹ scale — source: probes/results.md §6.
- "That is exactly why §10.4 caches it per (token, slice, pin), and the number makes the consequence concrete: if it ever drifts onto the per-viewport path, the system dies." — source: probes/results.md §6.
- The permutation must stay cached (DECIDED, serving-layer): "Permuting a 69M-item mask from entity to row space costs 8.8 s at 10⁹. §10.4 already requires caching it per (token, slice, pin); the number makes the consequence concrete — if it ever drifts onto the per-viewport path, the system is dead." — source: probes/optimisations.md §3.2.
- Signature-sorted entity allocation must be in the Phase 1 allocator, not added later, because "I9 makes entity-ID assignment permanent, so shipping created-order in the walking skeleton locks in an uncompressed index until a full re-allocation rebuild" — source: probes/optimisations.md §2.1.
- Entity ordering is not free from arrival order: "created-order entity IDs give run lengths of 1.00–1.26 against a 1.000 random baseline, i.e. nothing." — source: probes/optimisations.md §2.1.
- Signature-sorted entity allocation gains, simulated (postings total / 25% fragment): categories-subclass "7.7 → 0.9 MB (8.9×)" and "303 → 47 KB (6×)"; surnames "44.8 → 45.5 MB (1.0×)" and "303 → 303 KB (1×)"; hash t=100 "552.9 → 15.1 MB (36.7×)" and "303 KB → 0.2 KB (1,614×)" — source: probes/results.md §4.4.
- "These are floors on the scale effect: at 2.4M the mean categories group is 44 entities, so runs are short; at 10⁹ groups are ~400× longer and index size converges on O(Σ|signature| × batches) — a function of policy complexity, not corpus size. Entity space only; the row-space mask stays scattered (§5)." — source: probes/results.md §4.4.
- Signature-sorted allocation gain summary table: posting storage "8.9× (categories) to 36.7× (t=100)"; cached session fragment "6× to 1,614×"; mask union at equal coverage "up to 130×" — source: probes/optimisations.md §2.1.

## Morton ranking and quadtree tiles as contiguous row-ID ranges

- Coordinates are quantised onto a 2¹⁶ × 2¹⁶ grid and the bits of the two integer coordinates interleaved to produce a Morton (Z-order) code; items are sorted by this code and row IDs assigned as the rank in that order — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.2.
- Within a leaf tile the ordering is by priority rather than deeper Morton bits (§7.2) — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.2.
- Morton is chosen over Hilbert deliberately: "Hilbert has better locality, but Morton has the prefix property: each successive pair of bits identifies a quadrant, so every quadtree tile at every zoom level occupies exactly one contiguous range of codes and therefore of row IDs." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.2.
- "That single property underpins tile queries, density counts, LOD sampling and sharding. A precomputed tile table maps tile prefixes to rank ranges." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.2.
- "Note that a tile's identity is geometric — a Morton prefix — while its rank range is per-partition and per-slice. That is what lets partitions agree on tiles while ranking independently (§12.3)." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.2.
- "At 10⁹ items the grid gives 0.23 points per cell, so collisions are not a constraint. Past roughly 4 × 10⁹ the grid must widen with 64-bit codes." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §5.2.
- One file per column per segment is stored "in Morton-rank order with priority as the intra-leaf tiebreak," alongside a sorted `morton.u64` column, the tile table, and per-node candidate lists — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.3.
- "Tile → set of ranges in the interfaces — DECIDED. §11.3's multi-segment reality forces this regardless" — a single logical tile can correspond to multiple contiguous row ranges once multiple segments exist — source: probes/optimisations.md §3.3.
- Spatial autocorrelation under Morton order was measured directly (run ratio = mean run length of the mask in row order ÷ the 1/(1−p) expectation for a random mask of equal density): hash-flat all "1.00 (validates estimator)"; surnames head 25% "1.15"; surnames head 4.6% "1.05"; surnames random w=100 "1.03"; categories-subclass head 25% "2.32"; categories-archive head 25% "5.11" — source: probes/results.md §5.
- "The flat-hash control returns exactly 1.00, so the estimator and pipeline are sound and every other ratio is interpretable." — source: probes/results.md §5.
- "Realistic masks are essentially scattered. Surnames — the most realistic principal shape available — sits at 1.03–1.15. Categories reach only 1.7–2.3, and the archive head 5.11; those are the family the topic-correlation caveat says is flattered, so they are upper bounds." — source: probes/results.md §5.
- "Morton order buys no Roaring compression for any family: entity- and row-space serialisations are within noise." — source: probes/results.md §5.
- "The scaling analysis's uniform-scatter assumption — flagged as its weakest and conservative — is measured ≈true. Its residency and paging figures are forecasts, not floors. There is no hidden upside." — source: probes/results.md §5.
- "The topic-correlation caveat is quantified: category-derived terms overstate clustering by up to ~5× relative to orthogonal structure." — source: probes/results.md §5.
- Depth-6 tile duty cycle (% of occupied depth-6 tiles below the ~5% mask-coverage crossover, i.e. served by direct evaluation rather than candidate lists): surnames head 25% "1.8%"; surnames head 4.6% "63.3%"; surnames random w=100 "98.8%"; categories-subclass head 25% "30.8%"; categories-archive head 25% "38.9%" — source: probes/results.md §5 table.
- "Direct evaluation is the main selection path, with duty-cycle numbers: at working coverages 12–99% of occupied depth-6 tiles fall below the ~5% crossover; for tail-only principals essentially all do. Candidate lists serve only the dense cores of head principals." — source: probes/results.md §5.

## Roaring bitmap container structure

- "Roaring bitmap. A set of integers over a bounded universe, split into blocks of 2¹⁶, each block independently encoded as a sorted 16-bit array, a literal bit array or run-length pairs." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md (glossary, line ~687).
- "rank and select are not constant-time — both cost O(blocks before the position) — which is why §10.4 batches selections from a computed base rank." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md (glossary, line ~687).
- "Scatter costs run containers, not correctness: a fully scattered mask falls back to fixed-size bitmap containers, and range_cardinality cost depends on the range's span rather than the mask's density." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md (glossary, line ~687).
- "The term index maps each term to a Roaring bitmap of entity IDs. Roaring's per-block encoding makes the skewed size distribution harmless: head terms become run containers, the long tail small sorted arrays. Store terms below a few hundred members as plain sorted int32 arrays." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §6.2.
- Container type observed empirically at 10⁹ scale (categories-subclass, head 25%): "~1,900 postings per container (dense → bitmap containers, a fixed ~8 KB pass per merge, memory-bandwidth bound at ~5 GB/s)" — source: probes/results.md §4.3b.
- Container type observed empirically at 10⁹ scale (hash-flat): "~6.5 postings per container (sparse → array containers, tiny merges dominated by per-container dispatch)" — source: probes/results.md §4.3b.
- Small-term (sorted int32 array) representation is load-bearing, not an optimisation: at 117M terms, "34.4% of terms are singletons" and "the median posting is 3 entries"; as Roaring bitmaps that would be "~29 GB of container and object overhead alone"; as CSR it is "17.5 GB of postings plus 0.94 GB of offsets — inside Appendix A's ~20 GB budget" — source: probes/optimisations.md §2.2 and probes/results.md §4.3.
- "ClickHouse's threshold of 32 is the calibration point to start from." — source: probes/optimisations.md §2.2 (also .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §4.3 cites ClickHouse's threshold of 32).
- Universe-wide container count saturates at "15,259 — 10⁹/2¹⁶, every container in the universe — which is why a single 123k-posting term at 10⁹ already spans all of them." — source: probes/results.md §4.3b.

## The cost model: O(containers touched), not O(cardinality)

- "Bitmap operations cost O(containers touched), not O(cardinality)." Stated as the umbrella cost model from which "almost every headline in results.md is a corollary" — source: probes/optimisations.md §0.
- "A term with 100k postings spans ~15,259 containers when scattered over a 10⁹ universe and ~2 when contiguous — a ~7,500× difference in merge work for identical cardinality." — source: probes/optimisations.md §0.
- "Mask union at 25% coverage costs 21.7 ms or 2,885 ms depending only on posting shape, for the same 125 MB mask (§4.2)." — source: probes/optimisations.md §0.
- "Viewport counting is flat at 0.1–0.3 ms across every zoom level, because the mask has only ~15,000 containers and any set of ranges is bounded by one traversal (§6)." — source: probes/optimisations.md §0.
- "Dictionary scale is free on the authorise path: 117M terms costs the same as 10M, because union reads granted postings, not the vocabulary (§4.3)." — source: probes/optimisations.md §0.
- "Contiguity in entity space is the single highest-leverage property in the index — everything in §2 below is a way of buying it. And any benchmark that varies cardinality while holding container structure constant will mislead; the ratios that matter come from varying shape." — source: probes/optimisations.md §0.
- "This is a counting engine, not an aggregation engine. Counts, densities, existence, membership and samples are O(containers) — effectively free, independent of how many items are involved. Sums, means and percentiles are O(visible items), because the values must be gathered." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md (line ~885).
- "No bitmap construction recovers this: per-term partial sums double-count, since an item carries several terms and terms do not partition the corpus. Masked counting scales to 10⁹; a masked global mean over 10⁸ visible items is a scan." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md (line ~885).

- "Union cost is linear in containers spanned, with per-container cost drifting up only ~2× across 400× of scale (memory hierarchy). - "But there are two regimes, and the per-container constant differs by 13×. categories-subclass at 10⁹, head 25%: 125,279 containers, 193.1 ms = 1.54 µs/container, against hash-flat's 114 ns." — source: probes/results.md §4.3b.
- "So the refined model is Σ over container merges of work per merge, where a bitmap merge is a fixed 8 KB pass and an array merge is proportional to its elements. This sharpens §4.2's two-algorithm recommendation: concatenate-and-sort wins in the sparse-array regime (Roaring pays dispatch overhead for a handful of elements) and Roaring union wins in the dense-bitmap regime. The crossover is essentially Roaring's own array/bitmap threshold, which is a more principled heuristic than postings-per-container alone." — source: probes/results.md §4.3b.
- The kernel needs two algorithms, not one (DECIDED, serving): "Roaring n-way union costs O(terms × containers spanned); concatenating sorted posting arrays, radix sorting and bulk-constructing costs O(total postings). Neither dominates — the crossover moved by 400× across the configs measured." — source: probes/optimisations.md §1.2.
- "For many small scattered terms (the surnames random w=10⁴ shape: 308k postings, 93.5 ms by union) concatenation should win outright." — source: probes/optimisations.md §1.2.
- "Choose on: container density — concatenate-and-sort when a granted term's postings average few per container (Roaring pays per-container dispatch for a handful of elements), Roaring union when containers are dense (its fixed 8 KB bitmap pass is bandwidth-efficient). Measured, the per-container constant differs by 13× between those regimes (114 ns sparse vs 1.54 µs dense)." — source: probes/optimisations.md §1.2.

## On-disk layout

- "By the time the store is touched, every selection decision has been made in bitmap space. What arrives is an explicit sorted list of row IDs and a request for a gather: no query to plan, no predicate to push down, no join to optimise." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.1.
- "The survey in Appendix D found no existing engine that both retains a caller-supplied selection across queries and exposes a range-restricted cardinality over it." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.1.
- "Artifacts live in the bucket under immutable versioned prefixes; serving nodes sync the slices they need to instance-local NVMe at boot and mmap from there, so cold start is seconds. Compress at rest; decompress once at load." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.2.
- "Immutable prefixes make rebuilds atomic: write a new version, flip a pointer, roll back by flipping it back. The prefix name is the segment-set version in I11. This is also what makes a repartitioning (§12.5) expensive but not risky." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.2.
- "The organising property is that the row ID is the array index. Nothing is stored to locate row i; it lives at byte offset i × width." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.3.
- "One file per column per segment: raw little-endian, fixed-width, uncompressed, in Morton-rank order with priority as the intra-leaf tiebreak. Alongside them a sorted morton.u64 column, the tile table, and the per-node candidate lists." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.3.
- "Use the Arrow IPC file format with uncompressed buffers: self-describing, and the buffers remain page-aligned raw arrays that can be mmap'd and sliced zero-copy." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.3.
- Fold-back note: "frozen mask buffers need 32-byte alignment, a bundle-format requirement" (Architecture §4.1) — source: probes/optimisations.md §6 (item 5).
- Pair relations are stored "sorted, delta-encoded": "Sorting by (term_id, entity_id) and encoding with DELTA_BINARY_PACKED gives 3.8× smaller and 3× faster to read than unsorted. Sorting alone buys nothing — it is the combination." — source: probes/optimisations.md §2.4.
- Pair-relation encoding "applies to the bundle's pairs.arrow as well as to the test corpus, but note the stakes: after §1.1 that file is read to rebuild postings and by the oracle, not on either request path. This is bundle size and rebuild time." — source: probes/optimisations.md §2.4.

## The query path

- "Mask loading. Write mask fragments in CRoaring's frozen format and take a frozen_view over the mmap'd bytes — zero deserialisation, zero allocation. Deserialising a bitmap per query costs in proportion to mask cardinality rather than viewport size, which is the wrong asymptotic shape for panning." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- Named primitives the design depends on: "roaring_bitmap_range_cardinality for tile counts; roaring_bitmap_and_cardinality for masked counts without materialising an intersection; roaring_bitmap_rank plus roaring_bitmap_select for positioned access; roaring_bitmap_range_uint32_array to write directly into a gather buffer; roaring_bitmap_intersect_with_range to cull empty ranges before counting." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Version pinning. A request resolves its (segment-set version, watermark) pin once and uses it for the tile table, columns, permutation, candidate lists and mask alike (I11). Compaction publishes a new prefix and flips a pointer; in-flight requests complete against the old prefix, retained until drained. This is the standard session-pinning pattern from mature search engines." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Per-viewport path. A viewport resolves to a few hundred tiles. For each, two lookups give the [lo, hi) row range and range_cardinality gives the exact authorised count — no data file touched. Representatives come from the candidate list path of §7.2." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Collect selected row IDs across tiles into one u32 array. Processing tiles in Morton order yields it already sorted, which matters: a sorted gather is forward-sequential-with-gaps and cooperates with kernel readahead. The mmap touch is then tight loops writing directly into the output buffers, which are the Arrow arrays." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Paging. A tile of 10,000 rows spans ten 4 KB pages in one float32 column, so selecting thirty representatives touches at most ten pages. Column-major wins decisively for dense range reads — ten pages per column against forty-nine for the full row — and Arrow is columnar, so a row-major store would require a transpose on every response." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Structural ordering. The mask is the sole entry point to the geometry arrays: no counting, aggregating, hulling or density path may read a column except through a masked row-ID set, and none may be composed underneath the mask. This is I2 expressed as a code-structure rule rather than as a behavioural obligation, and it is the difference between an invariant that survives refactoring and one that erodes." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "The precedent is instructive — the one surveyed system that never leaks aggregates over unauthorised records gets that property purely from stacking every aggregating operator above its visibility filter, while the systems that do leak have a separate aggregation path that fell out of sync with the filtering one (Appendix D)." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Warmup. Pre-faulting at boot makes the mmap effectively resident for the process lifetime, which is the real argument for mmap over an explicit cache: no eviction policy to write or tune." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- "Expected latency, warm: low single-digit milliseconds per viewport with a single segment. Roaring's select walks containers from the start of the bitmap rather than being constant-time, so per-tile selections must be batched from a computed base rank." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.4.
- Node model: "A warm stateful tier is mandatory. Masking cannot be pushed to a CDN, unmasked tiles cannot be served for client-side filtering, and tiles cannot be sharded by term when principals satisfy thousands of them." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.5.
- "Serving nodes hold the term index, the auth plugin's auxiliary structures and the text index resident for their partition. Token-to-mask state is held with LRU plus maximum-lifetime eviction, with the auth data retained alongside so eviction is transparent (§2.3)." — source: .ignore/tessera-architecture-design.md §10.5.
